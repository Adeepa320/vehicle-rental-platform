# Cross-border transfer register

**Status:** v0.1 draft (2026-10-17), Phase 10. Owner: founder until a privacy lead is named ([PLACEHOLDER: DPO_CONTACT]). Every row: **LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED**.
**Why this exists.** The platform is established in Sri Lanka and its users are mostly in Sri Lanka, but every selected hosting and e-mail vendor processes data outside Sri Lanka. The Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022, as amended by Act No. 22 of 2025, treats "any other territory or country other than Sri Lanka" as a third country. Part III (which contains section 26 on cross-border data flows) comes into operation on **1 January 2027** (Gazette Extraordinary No. 2498/16 of 22 July 2026; verified 2026-10-17 on dpa.gov.lk).
**Related:** [PROCESSOR_REGISTER.md](PROCESSOR_REGISTER.md), [../security/DATA_RETENTION.md](../security/DATA_RETENTION.md), docs/SECURITY_AND_PRIVACY.md §7, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §13.

## What the law says (verified text, no interpretation)

- New section 26(1) (Amendment Act s.10): a controller or processor "may engage in cross-border data flows, only where such controller or processor, ensures compliance with the provisions of Part I, Part II and sections 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 and 25".
- Section 26(2): the controller or processor "shall adopt such instruments as may be specified by a directive issued by the Authority under paragraph (c) of section 33, to ensure binding and enforceable commitments of the recipient in the third country".
- Section 26(3): notwithstanding the above, cross-border flows are permitted where (a) the data subject has explicitly consented after being informed of the possible risks; (b) the transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract; (c) legal claims; (d) public interest; (e) emergency; (f) transit only; (g) as prescribed.
- The adequacy-decision mechanism of the original Act was removed (Amendment s.13 repeals s.53(2)(b)).
- The **only** published list of instruments is a **draft** Directive on Specification of Instruments (public consultation, 02-10-2024), directive 4: "(a) binding corporate rules; (b) an agreement; (c) a code of conduct; (d) a binding certification scheme; (e) a cross border processing impact assessment; and (f) a resolution of the board of directors". No final directive is published on dpa.gov.lk as of 2026-10-17.

**This register does not assert that any transfer mechanism is lawful or sufficient.** It records the facts of each flow and the safeguards the vendor offers, so counsel can choose and document the instrument. Nothing below is legal advice. LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED.

## Flows that do not leave Sri Lanka

- Platform team access from Sri Lanka to the hosted systems (the data is already abroad; the access itself is not a transfer out of Sri Lanka).
- Customers and providers who are abroad (tourists) accessing their own data: the data subject initiates the access; not recorded here (LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED if counsel disagrees).

## Open points for counsel

1. Which instrument from the draft directive (or its final version, when published) will the platform adopt for each vendor, and must a copy be filed with the Authority (draft directive 11 and 12: copies on written request, duty starting three years after section 26 commences)?
2. Does a board resolution (draft directive 4(f)) suffice for a small company during validation, with vendor DPAs as the underlying agreements?
3. Is the Cloudflare R2 unspecified-location storage acceptable for verification documents, or must documents move to a provider with a guaranteed Singapore region?
4. Does the Privacy Notice need an explicit consent step for cross-border processing (s.26(3)(a)) as a fallback, and what are the consequences for users who refuse?
5. Are EU Standard Contractual Clauses in vendor DPAs (Resend, Cloudflare, Sentry) recognised as "an agreement" ensuring binding and enforceable commitments under s.26(2)?
